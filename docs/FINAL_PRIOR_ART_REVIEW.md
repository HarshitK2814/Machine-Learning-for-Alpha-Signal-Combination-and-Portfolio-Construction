# Prior-art and bibliography review (2026-10-14, sprint 2)

Internal researcher-led review; not an external review. Method: lawful, publicly reachable sources only (publisher and repository pages, Crossref and arXiv records, author or institutional PDFs). Where a PDF was reachable it was downloaded to a scratch directory, text-extracted locally and searched; comparisons below rest only on text actually read. **A work is marked "full text read" only if its body text was read in the version named, which is often a working-paper or author version, not the published article.** Nothing was paid for, no access was circumvented, and nothing was inferred from memory or from general knowledge: where a source could not be read the limitation is stated. Credentials were not used. Metadata fetched through a summarising web tool were taken at face value from Crossref/arXiv records and recorded in `submission_candidate_as_run_only/BIB_PROVENANCE.csv`.

## 1. Closest prior art: Avramov, Cheng and Metzker (Management Science 2023) - full text read (updated 2026-10-14, gate 1)

**Access and verification.** Source consulted: the author-hosted PDF `https://si-cheng.net/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/2023-ms-avramov_cheng_metzker-machine-learning-vs.-economic-restrictions.pdf`, accessed 2026-10-14, downloaded to a scratch directory outside the repository and text-extracted locally; **the PDF itself is not committed** (copyrighted). It is the publisher-typeset article (INFORMS download stamp dated 9 May 2023, file name `MNSC20224449_proof.pdf`): 34 PDF pages = a download cover page plus the 33 journal pages 2587-2619; the title, authors, volume, issue, page range and DOI printed on its cover match the cited record (Doron Avramov, Si Cheng, Lior Metzker, *Management Science* 69(5), 2587-2619, 2023, DOI 10.1287/mnsc.2022.4449; received 2021-04-22, accepted 2021-09-26, online 2022-06-29). The whole main text, endnotes and reference list were read; the numeric cells of the tables were not transcribed. **Not accessed:** the online appendix and data files (Tables IA1-IA9, the equal-weighted results and the unreported results the text refers to), hosted at the DOI's supplemental-material link. Statements below about content that the text says is only in the appendix or "unreported" are therefore not verified.

**Page-level notes** (journal page numbers, mapped from the page footers of the extracted text): introduction and contribution statement, 2587-2589; models and data, 2590-2591 (NN3, the CPZ adversarial network, IPCA, a conditional autoencoder, a ridge SDF; CRSP/Compustat NYSE/AMEX/Nasdaq, 94 characteristics, predictors 1957-2017, out-of-sample 1987-2017); economic-restriction subsamples, 2592; return predictability and robustness (retraining on subsamples, alternative loss), 2592-2596; downside risk, turnover and costs, 2596-2603 (break-even costs on p. 2601, the Brandt-Santa-Clara-Valkanov cost function on p. 2602, the "do not imply unprofitable for all traders" passage on p. 2603); SDF-implied tangency weights, 2603; market states, 2604-2607; recent years, 2607; characteristics and industry decomposition, 2608-2614; conclusion, 2615-2616; endnotes including the price-impact caveat (endnote 29), 2616-2618.

**What the full text changes.** (i) The abstract-level summary in the candidate was incomplete in one respect that matters: the paper explicitly does **not** claim that ML is unprofitable for all traders, and it finds IPCA robust to restrictions, so "consistent with" is the strongest defensible wording. (ii) It owns the "restrict to cheap-to-trade names and value-weight" protocol for the US, so our investable-universe rule is not novel as a practice. (iii) It confirms that its cost treatment is proportional and capital-independent, which is the gap between it and an AUM-indexed cost model; but that gap is already occupied for the US by Jensen et al. (iv) It offers no tax, capacity, participation-cap or non-US analysis, so those dimensions are not covered by this paper.
