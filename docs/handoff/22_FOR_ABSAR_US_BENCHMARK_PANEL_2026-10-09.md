## Why a US panel, and why it is the one thing only you can supply

Every competitor we need to be compared against is US: Novy-Marx & Velikov (RFS 2016), Korajczyk &
Sadka (JF 2004), Azevedo-Hoegner-Velikov, Avramov-Cheng-Metzker (Management Science 2023). We
withdrew our claim that our capacity frontiers are "10-100x below US estimates" because the
comparison was not like-for-like, and that left the three non-US numbers with **no benchmark we
control**. Running the identical pipeline on CRSP/Compustat would give one:

1. **Method validation.** If our implementability frontier and cost model, run on US data,
   reproduce the published ordering (low-turnover strategies in the tens of $bn, high-turnover in
   the hundreds of $mn), the three non-US results inherit that credibility. If it does not, we find
   out before a referee does.
2. **A like-for-like capacity comparison**, which is the only way to make "non-US markets are
   shallower" a claim rather than an assertion.
3. **A fourth statutory regime for the tax placebo.** US long-term/short-term capital gains give a
   wedge-bearing jurisdiction with a very different turnover profile from India's.
4. **A cost-input coverage control.** US coverage is near complete, so it is the clean case for the
   coverage-asymmetry methods point (our retracted t = -6.10).

## What this will and will not do

It will **not** rescue a top-tier headline by itself: the direction of our main result already
appears in Novy-Marx-Velikov and Korajczyk-Sadka. What it can do is turn a JFQA paper whose
benchmarks are asserted into one whose benchmarks are measured, and it is the cheapest route to
finding out whether a stronger framing exists. If the US run shows something qualitatively
different (for example a small-AUM regime where the cap hurts), that would be new.
