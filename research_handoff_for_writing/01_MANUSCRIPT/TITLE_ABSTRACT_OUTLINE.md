# Title, abstract and section outline (extracted from manuscript.md)

**Title:** Costs, capacity and capital-gains tax for machine-learning equity strategies in Germany, India and Japan: an as-run measurement

**Abstract:**

We measure how much of a machine-learning equity signal survives three implementation frictions (AUM-indexed trading costs, an ADV participation constraint and statutory capital-gains tax) in Germany, India and Japan over 2009-2019, on identical signals and a common investable universe, using a dated lot-level tax engine. Four statements are supported by the traceable as-run evidence. (1) The gross decile composites have large Sharpe ratios before costs, but the executable participation-capped decile book has a negative net Sharpe ratio in all three markets at a $1bn mandate. Where a net Sharpe ratio of the optimised book is traceable (India, Japan), it is not distinguishable from zero. (2) The effect of the participation constraint, measured as an annualised net-return difference B(A) against an uncapped plan, increases monotonically with mandate size on the 35 rungs where the comparator is solvent; at large rungs the comparator is near-ruined and B(A) is an accounting difference against an infeasible plan, not a gain. (3) The statute decides whether a holding-period tax channel can exist: the long-term share of gains is exactly zero under the German and Japanese flat regimes (a mechanical property of those statutes that also serves as a check on the tax engine); on the executable decile book the tax channel is eight to nineteen times larger in the flat regimes than in India, which is itself borderline significant. (4) No machine-learning design axis (representation, conditioning, uncertainty) is detectable at the available power on the capped decile book in Germany and India. We also document a cost-input coverage asymmetry that manufactured a highly significant but spurious cross-country result. All numbers were produced under the project's original exit treatment (`as_run`); a revised analysis under the later exit ruling is blocked by missing data and was not run, so sensitivity to the exit treatment is unknown.
